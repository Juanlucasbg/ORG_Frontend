## Source 4 — Ganssle.com: "A Trillion Lines of Code"

URL: [https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
**Access status**: HTTP 403 — facts sourced from search-engine snippets and the mirrored version at embedded.com.
**Author**: Jack G. Ganssle, lecturer and consultant on embedded development.
**Canonical mirror**: [https://www.embedded.com/a-trillion-lines-of-code/](https://www.embedded.com/a-trillion-lines-of-code/)

- **Headline thesis**: Combining COBOL, PC code, web/Java, firmware, and other code domains, the total volume of code in the world is **approximately 1 trillion lines**. [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
- **Gartner 1997 COBOL estimate**: In **1997**, the **Gartner Group** estimated there were **~240 billion lines of COBOL** running active business applications worldwide. [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
- **COBOL production-rate assumption**: If those ~240 billion lines were written at a constant rate over the **40-year** history of the language (to 1997), developers produced approximately **6 billion lines of COBOL per year**. [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
- **Accounting for deprecated code**: Ganssle estimates that, adding all COBOL that no longer exists (retired applications), the world has likely been producing **~10 billion COBOL lines per year** over the language's history. [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
- **Workforce assumption**: For 40 years, approximately **1 million COBOL developers** were employed annually; at an estimated **10,000 LOC/person/year** (≈800 lines/person/month), this yields the ~10 billion lines/year figure. Ganssle notes this is an "unusually high productivity figure." [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
- **Broader code universe**: The trillion-line total aggregates COBOL alongside PC applications, web/Java code, and embedded firmware. The point is that no single language or platform accounts for the whole. [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
- **Methodological caveat**: Ganssle presents these as back-of-envelope estimates, not empirical measurements; the figures are explicitly reasoning exercises, not surveyed data. [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
- **Key quote**: "Combining Cobol, PC code, web Java, firmware and all the rest, it's not unreasonable to assume there's a trillion lines of code mediating the electronic hum that powers the world." [(ganssle.com)](https://www.ganssle.com/rants/atrillionlinesofcode.htm)
